What does this experience reveal about the person behind the application?

There is usually more to a student than their first draft.

A strong activity can become a generic essay. A thoughtful student can have difficulty explaining why something matters. A family can have plenty of university information and still be unsure what to do next.

My work is to close those gaps: between an experience and what it means, between an interest and a direction worth exploring, and between a pile of information and one useful next step.

How I work

Student ownership matters.

The experiences, ideas, and final voice belong to the student. My role is to guide choices, ask useful questions, check what a draft claims, and make the next revision more purposeful. Revisions are precise enough to act on without replacing the student's words.

What I check before a draft is final

The same standard applies whether I am reviewing one essay or a full set of supplements.

01

The prompt is actually answered

A vivid opening or an impressive list of courses can still leave the central question open. The essay has to respond to what was asked.

02

The right experience carries the essay

Not the achievement that sounds most impressive, but the one that gives a reader the clearest understanding of the student.

03

Claims hold up

Programs, courses, opportunities, and facts are checked. An opportunity the student hopes to pursue is not written as access already guaranteed.

04

The application agrees with itself

The personal statement, supplements, and activities list do not erase or contradict one another.

05

The reflection is visible

What changed, what the student noticed, and why it matters to them, rather than an account of winning an argument.

06

The voice is still the student's

Feedback is specific enough to act on without replacing the student's wording. The final essay is in the student's words.

US and UK applications

My casework to date is US-focused: Common App personal statements, UC Personal Insight Questions, and supplemental essays. I also offer UCAS personal statement review for UK applications. I have completed foundational UCAS training and understand predicted grades, the application mechanism, and how the UK's academic focus differs from US holistic review. The editorial discipline carries over, choosing the right evidence, checking claims, and keeping the student's voice, applied to the academic emphasis UCAS asks for.

About

A background in storytelling. A focus on education.

I studied Film/Video Production at Hampshire College after attending an international school in Shanghai, where I completed the IB Diploma after IGCSE. Having studied in both an international school and a US liberal arts college shapes how I think about identity, communication, and the questions students face when considering study abroad.

My experience spans independent English teaching and admissions writing, college counseling at Kang Chiao International School's Xiugang campus, educational-technology project work, and freelance video production. The common thread is helping people communicate an idea and move a piece of work forward. Alongside editorial work, I am continuing to build toward college counseling.
